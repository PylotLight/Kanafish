# Kanafish’s 12-week conversation and kana course

This is an original, independent beginner-to-low-intermediate companion broadly aligned to the scope and sequence of *GENKI I* (Third Edition). It uses new scenes, explanations, prompts, and dialogues; it does not reproduce textbook text, exercises, or audio and is not affiliated with the publisher. The three short lessons each week keep practical conversation at the centre.

The first two weeks introduce the full core hiragana set and katakana alongside conversation, in the same early sequence as *GENKI I*. Learners practise a few kana groups at a time and revisit them later: this is a first pass, not a one-week mastery deadline, and script practice never gates lesson progress.

| Week | Conversation focus | Language and script goals |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meet people and introduce yourself | Polite です sentences, yes/no questions, は・の・も, simple negative answers; practise core hiragana, voiced sounds, and common contracted combinations. |
| 2 | Ask about things and shop | これ・それ・あれ and この・その・あの, asking whose item and how much; practise core katakana, voiced sounds, combinations, small ッ, long vowels, and common foreign-word sounds. |
| 3 | Talk about routines and make plans | Polite non-past verbs, time and place particles, frequency, invitations with 〜ませんか; reuse familiar kana in everyday activities. |
| 4 | Talk about places and past events | あります・います, simple place relationships, polite past forms, quantities and duration; read familiar place and time words in kana. |
| 5 | Describe a trip and make choices | Common い/な adjectives, preferences with 好き, suggestions with ましょう, and familiar counters; keep kanji selective and supported by kana readings. |
| 6 | Connect actions and ask permission | て-form requests, linking actions, permission and simple rules, short reasons and offers of help. |
| 7 | Describe people and what they are doing | 〜ています, appearance and clothing, connected descriptions, family counts, and going somewhere to do an activity. |
| 8 | Enjoy a meal and talk casually | Plain forms with familiar friends, simple opinions and reported comments, activity preferences, and polite requests not to do something. |
| 9 | Retell a story and check what happened | Casual past and negative past, describing clauses, reported opinions, もう・まだ, and short reasons. |
| 10 | Compare options and make a winter plan | Comparisons, intentions with つもり, changes with なる, transport, travel time, and “somewhere / nowhere” choices. |
| 11 | Share wishes and past experiences | 〜たい, examples of activities, 〜たことがある, introductions, hometowns, and examples with や. |
| 12 | Explain a health concern and respond | 〜んです, gentle advice, 〜すぎる, reasons, and what needs to happen next; combine familiar language in a less-scripted conversation. |

## Milestone checkpoints

- **Week 2 — First conversation:** give a short self-introduction, ask two familiar questions, handle a simple object or price question, and identify key information in clear prompts.
- **Week 4 — Places and past events:** locate a person or object, tell a short account of a past visit, revisit an earlier shopping function, and catch key details.
- **Week 6 — Requests and daily life:** make a polite request, ask permission or check a rule, connect actions with a reason, and understand the key action and reason.
- **Week 8 — People and casual conversation:** describe a person or activity, share an opinion with a friend, and choose polite or casual language to suit the scene.
- **Week 10 — Comparison and plans:** compare options, state a plan, confirm a travel or time detail, and ask for repetition rather than guessing.
- **Week 12 — Integrated conversation:** sustain a short familiar exchange across several functions, ask follow-up questions, use a repair phrase, and understand the gist and key facts of clear beginner speech.

These checkpoints record practice and observable evidence, not fluency. They can be completed with a partner, tutor, or self-recording; automated speech recognition is not required.
